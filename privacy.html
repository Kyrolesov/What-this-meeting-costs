Privacy Policy

What this meeting costs — Apple Watch app · Last updated 19 October 2026

This app records your purchases, and the identifiers that come with them. There is no account, no usage analytics and no tracking. Your rate and your receipts stay on your Apple Watch and are never sent anywhere. What the app does send — to Apple for purchases, and to RevenueCat with an anonymous ID and a device identifier — is described below.

What is stored, and where

Everything the app remembers is stored on your watch, in the app's own storage:

  • the pay rate you entered, its currency, period and working-week settings;
  • your receipts — the amount, duration, rate, room size and target of each finished session;
  • the meeting presets you saved — a name, a room size and an optional target;
  • display and haptics preferences;
  • whether the one-time purchase has been unlocked, cached so the app works offline;
  • a small snapshot of the running session, shared with the app's own watch-face complication so it can draw the amount.

None of this leaves the device. It is not backed up to any service we operate, because we operate none.

Network use

All network use is about purchases, and none of it carries what you typed: no rate, no receipts, no presets, no name and no Apple Account.

When the app starts, it asks the App Store for the two products and their prices in your currency, and asks again on returning to the screen only if the prices have not arrived yet. Every time the app starts or comes back to the screen, the RevenueCat library checks in with its own service: it fetches its settings, the list of products, and the purchase status of this installation.

When you buy the one-time unlock, leave a tip, or restore a previous purchase, Apple performs the transaction through the App Store; we never see your payment details, your Apple Account, or anything else about you. The RevenueCat library then reports the transaction — product, price, currency, date. It also reports, by itself, a purchase it finds on the watch that it has not reported yet — for example after the app is reinstalled — and every change the App Store announces to the watch: a purchase restored, shared through Family Sharing, or refunded.

Every request to RevenueCat carries:

  • an anonymous identifier the library creates for this installation and keeps;
  • the identifier Apple gives this app on this watch (the identifier for vendor), which is the same for every app from the same developer and is not the advertising identifier;
  • the watch model, watchOS version, language and region settings, App Store country, and the app's name and version;
  • the IP address that any request over the internet carries, which RevenueCat uses to work out the App Store country and does not keep.

If their service is unreachable, the app behaves exactly the same: purchases are verified by Apple on the watch, not by RevenueCat. A tip is reported the same way, and it still unlocks nothing — the app does not remember whether you left one, and cannot tell you.

Third parties

One: RevenueCat, which receives the requests described above and nothing else. We use it to see sales totals. Purchases themselves are verified and delivered by Apple — RevenueCat is bookkeeping, not a gatekeeper. There is no advertising library and no usage analytics library.

Tracking

The app does not track you across apps or websites and does not use the advertising identifier. Its privacy manifest declares no tracking. In the App Store privacy label, the purchase records and the identifiers above appear as collected, not linked to your identity and not used for tracking.

Children

The app is rated 4+. It treats everyone the same way, of any age: what it sends is described above, and nothing else.

Deleting your data

Two ways, both immediate and both entirely under your control:

  • in the app, open Settings → Data and delete all receipts — or erase everything, which also removes the rate, the presets and every preference and returns the app to its first-launch screen. A purchase is not erased: it belongs to your Apple Account, not to the watch;
  • delete the app from your Apple Watch, which removes everything it stored on the watch.

Your rate, receipts and presets exist nowhere but the watch, so there is no copy of them for us to delete on your behalf. One thing outlives deleting the app: the purchase records held by RevenueCat, which stay on their service until they are deleted there. They are not linked to your name or Apple Account; if you want them gone anyway, write to the address below and we will have them deleted.
